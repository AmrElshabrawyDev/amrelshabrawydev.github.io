"How much will my website cost?" is the first question almost every client asks me — and the honest answer is *it depends on what the website has to do*. That's not a dodge. A one-page site for a clinic and a store that takes payments are completely different jobs, even if both are "a website".

This guide breaks down what actually drives the price of a Next.js website, gives you realistic budget ranges, and shows you how to get a quote you can trust.

## Why Next.js (and why it can cost more than a template)

[Next.js](https://nextjs.org) is the React framework used by companies like Nike, Notion and TikTok. For a business, it means three things:

- **Speed.** Pages are pre-built and served instantly, which helps both conversions and Google rankings (Core Web Vitals).
- **SEO control.** Every page can have its own title, description, canonical URL and structured data.
- **No plugin bloat.** You get exactly the features you need — nothing slowing the site down, nothing to "update or get hacked".

The trade-off: a custom Next.js site is *built*, not *installed*. You're paying for development time instead of a $59 theme. For many businesses that's worth it; for some, a simpler tool is enough (I'll tell you if that's you).

## The 6 things that decide the price

### 1. Number of unique page designs

Ten pages that share one layout cost far less than ten pages that each need their own design. When you ask for a quote, count **templates**, not pages: home, service page, blog post, contact, and so on.

### 2. Design: Figma file or from scratch?

If you already have a design in Figma, the job is "turn this into a fast, responsive website" — very predictable. If you need design too, add design time and at least one feedback round.

### 3. Content and languages

A bilingual Arabic/English site is not just "translate the text". RTL layout, fonts, and SEO for both languages all need work. Also ask yourself honestly: *is the content ready?* Waiting for content is the #1 reason projects run late.

### 4. Dynamic features

This is where budgets grow the most:

| Feature | Impact on cost |
| --- | --- |
| Contact form, WhatsApp button, analytics | Small |
| Blog managed by you (CMS or admin panel) | Medium |
| User accounts and login | Medium–large |
| Online payments, cart and checkout | Large |
| Dashboards, roles, custom business logic | Large |

### 5. Integrations

Payment gateways, CRMs, booking systems, shipping APIs, email services — each integration needs setup, testing and error handling.

### 6. SEO and migration work

If you're moving from an existing site that already ranks on Google, preserving those rankings is real work: URL inventory, redirects, metadata, monitoring. I wrote a full guide on [migrating WordPress to Next.js without losing SEO](/blog/wordpress-to-nextjs-migration-seo).

## Typical budget ranges

Freelance rates vary a lot by region and experience. According to [Lemon.io's 2026 rate data](https://lemon.io/rate-calculator/next-js-developers/), Next.js developers charge roughly **$26–$62 per hour** globally, while agencies usually bill considerably more because the rate includes project managers and a larger team.

Instead of hourly rates, most small-business projects are better priced as a **fixed quote**. As a rough guide to scope:

| Project type | Typical scope | Typical timeline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Landing page | 1 page, form, analytics, SEO basics | 1–2 weeks |
| Business website | 5–10 pages, 3–5 templates, blog | 2–4 weeks |
| Bilingual (Arabic/English) site | Business site + RTL + 2-language SEO | 3–5 weeks |
| Online store | Catalog, cart, checkout, payments | 4–8 weeks |
| Web app / dashboard | Auth, roles, data, custom logic | 6 weeks + |

> **Rule of thumb:** if two quotes for the "same" website are wildly different, the two developers understood different projects. Ask each one exactly what's included.

## Hidden costs to ask about before you sign

- **Hosting.** Many Next.js sites run for free or cheaply on Vercel or similar platforms; stores and apps with databases cost more monthly.
- **Domain and email.** Usually a small yearly cost, but make sure the domain is registered in *your* name.
- **Content.** Copywriting, photos and translation are often not included.
- **Maintenance.** Who fixes things after launch, and at what cost?
- **Ownership.** You should own the code, the domain and every account. Always.

## How to get an accurate quote (and a better price)

1. **Write down the goal**, not just the pages: "get more WhatsApp inquiries from Kuwait" is more useful than "5 pages".
2. **Share 2–3 websites you like** and say *what* you like about them.
3. **List must-have vs. nice-to-have features.** Launching with the must-haves first is the easiest way to cut cost.
4. **Have your content ready**, or budget for someone to write it.
5. **Ask for a fixed price with a written scope**, a timeline, and what happens if the scope changes.

## Is Next.js worth it for your business?

If your website is a real sales channel — people find you on Google, compare you with competitors, and contact you from the site — then speed, SEO and a polished experience pay for themselves. If you only need an online business card, a simpler solution may be enough.
